"Defense" rates how many fantasy points the opponent has allowed to that position over its last three games, compared with every defense playing this week. Players on injured reserve or the practice squad aren't projected. The official injury report is checked again Friday and Sunday morning: Q questionable, D doubtful, and players ruled out are hidden. Scorecard

Each week's projections are graded against what actually happened, alongside the benchmark: the player's own average over his last three games. A lower average miss is better. Only players who played are graded.

Research

Four longer investigations. Each started with a question, and several ended with a "no" that turned out to be the interesting part. Full code and reports are on GitHub.

About

I'm Luca Martini, a high school student in Dallas who likes asking whether the things people say about sports actually hold up in the data. This site collects that work: a live weekly projection model and four research projects across the NFL, college basketball, and European soccer.

How the projections work

  • For each player, the model looks at his last three games this season: fantasy points, targets, carries, receptions, and yards.
  • It adds how many points his next opponent has allowed to his position over its last three games.
  • A linear regression trained on every regular-season game since 2021 turns those numbers into a projection. Its main skill is regression to the mean: it trusts a three-game hot streak less than the raw average does.
  • Every Wednesday the site grades last week and posts the new projections automatically. Projections are locked once posted; only the injury flags update later in the week.

What it can't do

  • It skips players on injured reserve and flags the official injury report, but a projection doesn't change if a teammate is ruled out late in the week.
  • Rookies and players returning from injury need a game or two before they're projected.
  • Single games are noisy. Even a good model misses by four to five points on a typical player.

How it's built

Python with pandas and scikit-learn, data from the open-source nflverse project, and a scheduled GitHub Actions job that reruns everything weekly. I designed the questions, ran and checked the analysis, and wrote the conclusions; I used an AI coding assistant to help write and debug the code.
