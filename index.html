Where the model fails

Average miss by position, against the best simple method in the same weeks.

PositionMy modelBest simple methodBetter
  • Players who get hurt or benched mid-game. A quarterback pulled in the first half scores near zero, and nothing before kickoff predicts it. These make up most of the "big misses."
  • Breakout games. The model leans against streaks, so it is slow to believe a player whose role suddenly grows.
  • Matchups. The opponent feature adds almost nothing in testing. Treat the matchup note as context, not a reason to start someone.
  • Players coming back from an absence are projected and flagged "BACK." The model lowers them for their first game back, since many play limited snaps, but one game of rust is hard to call. Rookies still need a game before they're projected.
  • Late injury news. The Friday and Sunday updates use the official injury report, but game-day inactives come out 90 minutes before kickoff, after the last update.

About

I'm Luca Martini, a high school student in Dallas who likes asking whether the things people say about sports actually hold up in the data. This site collects that work: a live weekly projection model, a public record of how it performs, and research projects across the NFL, college basketball and European soccer.

How the projections work

  • For each player, the model (version 2, live since week 10) looks at his last three games (fantasy points, targets, carries, receptions and yards), his averages so far this season, and his average last season. It also sees how many points his next opponent has allowed to his position recently.
  • It is trained on every regular-season game since 2021 to predict each player's middle outcome: the score he should beat about half the time. Two more models give his 10th and 90th percentile, which make up the 80% range.
  • Its main skill is not overreacting. A three-game hot streak counts, but so do the season-long numbers behind it.
  • It also reads the team's situation for that game from the official injury report and rosters: whether the quarterback who's been starting is ruled out or a regular starter is back, and how much of the team's targets and carries belong to teammates at the same position who are out or returning. Players coming back from an absence are projected too, flagged "BACK".
  • Every Wednesday the site grades last week and posts the new projections automatically. Those are locked and graded. On Friday and Sunday morning the model reruns with the latest injury report, for games that haven't started; those updates are timestamped and graded separately.

Changelog

How it's graded

Every player who played is graded, with no exceptions for injuries or blowouts. The model is compared with four simple methods: the player's last game, his last three games, his season average, and a flat average for his position. The leaderboard shows all of them, including the weeks and positions where a simple method wins.

How it's built

Python with pandas and scikit-learn, data from the open-source nflverse project, and a scheduled GitHub Actions job that reruns everything weekly. I designed the questions, ran and checked the analysis, and wrote the conclusions; I used an AI coding assistant to help write and debug the code.
